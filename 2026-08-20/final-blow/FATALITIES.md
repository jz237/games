# Final Blow 1.8C Reality Break fatalities

Every Graphic Fatality gives all three cinematic beats to the fighter's
assigned personal projectile:

1. **Prime:** the object is launched, spun up or released in its own close-up.
2. **Trap/strike:** the object catches, bonds, scores or pins the chosen limb.
3. **Kill:** that same object visibly causes the severing impact. Its name,
   object art and object-specific VFX own the final shot and aftermath.

The restraint supports the assigned special instead of replacing it. One arm or
leg is explicitly severed by the special; the complete limb is thrown into the
scene, bounces once and remains beside the victim while the stump pumps blood.

The staging takes tonal influence from grim trap horror and 1990s arcade
Fatalities without reproducing a scene or device from another work.

## Reality Break art direction

Normal combat retains its fast arcade presentation. Executing a Final Blow now
crossfades the arena into an original 96 KB photoreal industrial-night environment
plate, then applies practical amber/blue split lighting, real material texture,
high-resolution fighter portrait detail, restrained film grain and a deeper
cinematic vignette. The full transition is complete before the first projectile
trap beat and stays active through the aftermath for all 16 finishers.

Reduced Motion shortens the crossfade and freezes the grain pattern; High
Contrast removes the portrait/grain detail layers. Neither option changes the
assigned-special choreography, limb result or deterministic simulation.
Like all game media, the environment plate loads on demand rather than joining
the PWA install shell; if it is unavailable offline, the same filmic lighting,
fighter-detail and vignette passes remain over the cached arena.

The **Graphic Fatalities** option remains the master safety gate. Turning it off
keeps the projectile camera and three-beat special timing but removes severed
limbs, stump wounds, blood spray, gore fragments, pools and lens splatter. The
assigned projectile remains visible because it is part of the move, not a gore
layer.
